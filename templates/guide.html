Decoder Guide

A METAR is a short coded weather report from an airport. Here's how to read one, group by group.

An example, piece by piece

METAR KJFK 241851Z 31012G18KT 10SM FEW045 BKN250 18/06 A3002 RMK AO2
GroupMeaning
METARRoutine hourly report. SPECI means a special report issued because conditions changed.
KJFKStation: the 4-letter ICAO code (New York JFK). US codes start with K, UK codes with EG.
241851ZDay 24 of the month, 18:51 Zulu (UTC).
31012G18KTWind from 310° at 12 knots, gusting to 18. 00000KT is calm and VRB is variable.
10SMVisibility 10 statute miles. Outside the US it's in meters, and 9999 means 10 km or more.
FEW045Few clouds at 4,500 ft. Heights are in hundreds of feet above the ground.
BKN250Broken clouds at 25,000 ft. The lowest BKN or OVC layer is the ceiling.
18/06Temperature 18 °C, dew point 6 °C. M means minus, e.g. M02.
A3002Altimeter 30.02 inHg. Outside the US it's Q1016, in hectopascals.
RMK AO2Remarks. AO2 is an automated station with a precipitation sensor.

Cloud cover

Cover is measured in eighths (oktas) of the sky.

SKC / CLRSky clear (CLR: no clouds below 12,000 ft, automated station)
FEWFew: 1–2 eighths
SCTScattered: 3–4 eighths
BKNBroken: 5–7 eighths. Counts as a ceiling.
OVCOvercast: 8 eighths. Counts as a ceiling.
VVVertical visibility into fog or other obscuration. Counts as a ceiling.
CB / TCUCumulonimbus / towering cumulus, e.g. BKN020CB

Weather codes

Weather is built from an intensity, a descriptor and a phenomenon. For example, -SHRA means light rain showers and +TSRA means a heavy thunderstorm with rain.

Values this app calculates

Relative humidity comes from the temperature and dew point (Magnus formula). When the two are close, expect fog or low cloud.

Density altitude is how high the airplane "feels" it is. Hot weather and low pressure raise it and reduce aircraft performance. It's estimated as pressure altitude + 120 ft × (temperature − standard temperature).

Headwind and crosswind for each runway come from the angle between the wind and the runway heading: headwind = wind × cos(angle), crosswind = wind × sin(angle).
